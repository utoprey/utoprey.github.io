About Me

Ekaterina Antipushina is a researcher and ML engineer working in NeuroAI and Embodied AI. She is a PhD student at Skoltech and a Machine Learning Engineer at the Spatial Intelligence Lab, Applied AI Institute. She develops multimodal machine learning methods for neural signals, images, and 3D scenes, with interests in foundation models, generative modeling, representation learning, and spatiotemporal modeling.

In NeuroAI , her work covers brain activity modeling, EEG and fMRI integration, cross-modal prediction, and real-time neurofeedback. Her projects include pyOpenNFT , an open-source framework for neurofeedback with machine learning, and CSTNet , a generative approach to EEG-to-ECoG mapping using optimal transport.

In Embodied AI , she focuses on spatial intelligence: using vision-language models to understand 3D scenes, locate objects from language descriptions, and reason about spatial relationships. Her work includes scene representations, 3D visual grounding, and evaluation of spatial reasoning. Details are available in her CV .

Her broader research includes medical computer vision, interpretable analysis of multimodal biomedical data, and collaborative studies of brain organoids and biomarkers. See her publications on Google Scholar .

Outside the lab, you'll find me in Moscow's cozy cafés seeking inspiration.

Research Talks

🧠

Universal Brain Encoder: The Evolution of Models for Neural Signals

NeuroTalk Meetup, MISIS University, Moscow, April 2026

An invited talk at the NeuroTalk meetup on neurotechnology and brain-computer interfaces. The talk traces how models for neural signals evolved from task-specific architectures towards universal brain encoders — foundation models trained across subjects, montages and recording modalities — and what this shift means for decoding and for brain-computer interfaces.

Event Announcement

Bridge Between Time and Space: How Generative AI Turns EEG into fMRI

DataFest 2025 (ODS), March 2025

The report is devoted to the application of generative models for the transformation of neuroimaging data with high temporal resolution (EEG) into functional and spatial representations (fMRI), combining their strengths. Modern algorithms, architectural solutions and examples of use in diagnostics and brain research are considered
